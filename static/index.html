How the preview draws a store

  • Cells as stored. Each cell of the store's own grid or mesh is drawn flat in one color: an unstructured mesh by its triangles, a grid by its cells. Nothing is smoothed or interpolated.
  • Records held. Each record is shown as it is; playback steps from one to the next without blending.
  • Fixed scale. A field's color scale is set once for the whole store, from the 1st to the 99th percentile of its values weighted by cell area (signed fields: symmetric about zero at the 99.5th percentile of the magnitude). Water speed uses a fixed log ramp from 0.02 to 3.1 m/s, so stores compare directly.
  • Layers. Layers run from the surface down, labeled with their mean depth. Sigma layers without the water depth in the store are labeled by sigma and stacked evenly in 3D.
  • Large grids. A grid with more cells than the page can hold is shown every n-th cell in each direction; each drawn cell carries one source cell's value, never an average. The legend says when.
  • Sections. Each sample along the line takes its host cell's value in every layer.
  • 3D. The surface cells see-through over a lit bed: a mesh's own faceted bed where the store holds the depth at its nodes, else each cell flat at its own bed with walls between them. Level maps (up to four) draw whole layers at their heights.
  • Tracers (3D). In a thinned set of columns and up to six levels down each, one segment per cell moves along that cell's velocity and wraps inside the cell, so it shows direction and speed without travelling past what the data holds. Colored by depth in the column.
